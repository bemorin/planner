Cookies and storage

Daily Planner sets no cookies. Your browser does keep some data so the planner can work — mostly your planner itself. Here is all of it.

No cookies

The planner, the welcome page and these pages set no cookies, and load no advertising or tracking scripts. The anonymous counts described in the privacy policy use no cookies either.

To count visits to the site’s pages, the pages load Cloudflare Web Analytics, which our host, Cloudflare, runs for us. It sets no cookies and keeps nothing in your browser, doesn’t fingerprint your device and doesn’t follow you to other sites: we only see totals, such as how many visits a page had and how fast it loaded. No consent is needed for it: it only measures this site’s audience, without cookies, anonymously and in totals, within the CNIL’s rules for audience measurement (see the privacy policy).

Our host, Cloudflare, can set a strictly necessary security cookie in some cases — for example __cf_bm when bot protection is on, or cf_clearance after a security check. They protect the site and are not used to follow you (Cloudflare’s list).

What your browser keeps

The planner saves data in your browser’s storage, on your device. None of it is sent to us. Keys that start with daily-planner: are your planner: they go into backups and, if you use it, the sealed copy of Personal sync, and so do your older days once they have moved to this browser’s database (below). The others belong to this browser only. Keys that earlier versions kept and this one no longer uses are removed the first time it opens.

The offline copy

When you open the planner, a service worker saves its files in your browser’s cache so it opens and works without a connection. Each new version replaces the previous copy. It holds the app and pages of this site, not your plans.

How to clear it

  • Your planner: in the planner, open Help → Data on this device → Reset local data. It removes your older days from this browser’s database too. Export a backup first if you may want it later.
  • Everything this site keeps, including your preferences and the offline copy: clear the site data for this address in your browser’s settings (usually under Privacy or Site settings). Your planner is deleted too, so back it up first.
  • Personal sync: disconnect in the planner’s Settings first. On this device, Disconnect removes the cloud’s access (daily-planner-sync:google-access and daily-planner-sync:google-account, or daily-planner-sync:dropbox-auth, which Dropbox is also asked to revoke) and, once no cloud is left connected, the seal: the key, the IndexedDB database daily-planner-sync that holds it, and daily-planner-sync:protection. What stays is about your planner, not your account: daily-planner-sync:cloud, daily-planner-sync:ledger, daily-planner-sync:google and daily-planner-sync:dropbox (the cloud you chose, what changed since the last sync and how it went, so that connecting again compares your days instead of overwriting them), and the recovery snapshots in IndexedDB daily-planner-recovery. Clear the site data to remove those too. The sealed copy in your cloud stays until you delete it there: in Google Drive, Settings → Manage apps → Daily Planner → Delete hidden app data; in Dropbox, the planner’s folder in your Apps folder, and its access under Settings → Connected apps.

Google and Dropbox

If you connect Google Drive, the planner loads Google’s sign-in and Google’s window opens on Google’s own pages, where Google uses its own cookies under its own policy. Nothing from Google loads before you choose Google Drive in the planner’s settings or reach for its Connect button: the planner then loads Google’s sign-in, so that Google’s window can open from your tap.

If you connect Dropbox, the planner takes you to Dropbox’s own page to allow access, then back. There, Dropbox uses its own cookies under its own policy. Nothing from Dropbox loads before you choose Connect.
